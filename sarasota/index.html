How Sarasota Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

A home usually gives a few hints before a leak turns serious. Our technicians see the same early clues again and again in Sarasota, and they are worth learning. Blistering paint, ceiling stains, warped baseboards and floorboards that curl at the edges each point to water behind or beneath them. Add a stale odor in an enclosed space, a loose toilet, or a faucet that keeps dripping.

Taken singly they are minor, though every one of them gets more expensive with time. Re-caulk tubs and showers when the bead cracks, since water behind tile does damage you will not see for months. Test a sump pump several times a year by filling the pit until the float trips and the motor runs. In basements and garages, shelving and plastic tubs keep belongings clear of the floor.

Good upkeep lowers the odds, but it cannot remove them. Fewer and smaller leaks are still a real gain, especially compared with a Sarasota home full of drying machines for most of a week.

Found Water in Your Sarasota Home? Start Here

What you do before help reaches your Sarasota address has a real effect on how the job goes. Look after people first and property second. If water is near outlets, cords or the electrical panel, stay out of it, and switch off power to that area only if you can reach the breaker without stepping in water. If the water is from a sewer or toilet backup, do not touch it at all.

Next, stop the source. Use the small valve behind the toilet or under the sink, or go straight to the main. After that, carry light items such as rugs, documents, electronics and chairs somewhere dry. Slide plastic or aluminum foil beneath the feet of anything too heavy to move, which stops stains and swelling.

Take photos and video of every affected room before you clean up, because your insurer will want to see the damage as it was. If the water is clean and shallow, blot what you can, and let our Sarasota crew handle everything else.

Water Damage We See Most in Sarasota

Some water problems need a phone call this minute, and others can safely wait until morning. The five below are ones we see constantly in Sarasota, listed from most pressing to least.

Bursts: A pipe that has split is the most urgent call there is, because it puts out water for as long as the supply is on. Cold weather, depending on the season, is one trigger, with the leak appearing as the frozen section thaws. Close the main valve, open a faucet to relieve pressure and call right away, even at night. Ceilings that bulge with trapped water should be left alone and the room below kept empty.

Runoff: Rain running into the house at ground level ranks a close second. You cannot stop the rain, but you can move belongings up and keep away from any outlets near the water. This water has crossed lawns, driveways and streets, which makes it unsanitary. The Coral Gables Restoration team extracts it once conditions are safe, then removes soaked materials that cannot be cleaned.

Kitchens: A dishwasher that leaks from its door or a refrigerator line that drips behind the unit calls for same-day attention. Turning off the supply valve for that appliance normally ends the leak itself. What remains is the water trapped below the cabinets and floor covering, and it will not dry by itself. For Sarasota kitchen jobs, our crew can frequently save the cabinets by pushing dry air in at the toe kick.

Crawlspaces: Moisture under a raised floor is seldom an emergency, but it should not be put off for a season. Puddles on the ground cover, dripping ductwork or wet insulation between the joists are what an inspection usually turns up. Left alone, the damp air rises into the rooms above and the wood framing begins to decay. Our crew clears any standing water, brings the framing down to a normal moisture level and reports the likely cause.

Odors: A musty smell near the site of an old leak is the least urgent item here, yet it deserves an inspection soon. Mold can begin within 24 to 48 hours of a material getting wet, so a leak from months ago had plenty of time. Avoid wiping or bleaching a big area on your own, as that sends spores into other rooms. We check for hidden dampness, set up containment and clear out the affected material for homeowners in Sarasota.
